About this planner

Where the data comes from, how accurate it is, and how to change it.

Data sources and attribution

The trail is not drawn by hand. It is the actual Tour du Mont Blanc main itinerary relation from OpenStreetMap — a single continuous closed loop of 736 ways and 16,999 points measuring 166.01 km — cut into daily legs by projecting each overnight stop onto it.

How accurate are the numbers?

Elevation was checked against surveyed col heights along the route. Mean absolute error is about 3.7 m: Col de la Seigne came out 0.2 m low against a surveyed 2,515 m, Col du Brévent 0.3 m high against 2,368 m, and the worst case was Grand Col Ferret at 8.7 m low.

Cumulative ascent is smoothed before being summed. Adding up every positive step in a raw elevation series inflates climbing badly, because metre-scale sampling noise accumulates over thousands of points, so a run of same-direction steps only counts once it exceeds 5 m. Computed ascent lands 1–7% below the group's original planning spreadsheet, which is the expected signature of that filter. Both figures are shown side by side rather than one overriding the other.

Walking times are computed segment by segment along the elevation profile rather than from a day's totals, because a day with one long climb takes considerably longer than a day with the same ascent spread thinly. Two models are available on the Planner: Naismith's rule with Langmuir's descent correction, and Tobler's hiking function. Where they disagree sharply the day has sustained steep ground and the estimate deserves to be read as a range.

Full calibration tables, including the two places where our plan and the geometry disagree, are in docs/data-notes.md.

Editing and sharing changes

There is no server and no database. The committed JSON files under data/ are the shared source of truth, and anything you change in the browser is kept as a draft in this device's local storage. That is why an unsaved badge appears in the header once you edit something — the change is real, but only here.

To share an edit with the group, open that badge, copy the JSON, and commit it over the matching file in the repository. Anyone else can pull the change, or use Import to load a file someone sent them. Discarding a draft returns the page to whatever is committed.

Drafts live in one browser on one device. Clearing site data will discard them, so export anything you want to keep.

Using it on the trail

Leaflet and all trip data are served from this site rather than a CDN, and a service worker caches the whole planner on first visit, so every page keeps working with no signal. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app.

Map tiles are the part that genuinely needs downloading ahead of time. Use the panel below over wifi before you leave, and the map still draws in the middle of the Val Ferret.

Every page works at phone width, and the elevation profile responds to touch — drag along it to read distance, height and elapsed time, and the marker tracks your finger on the map above.

Print a paper copy from the trail brief as a backup for a flat battery.
