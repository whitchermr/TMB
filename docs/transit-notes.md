# Transit notes

Where the transit data came from, how much of it to believe, and what has to be
re-checked before anyone gets on a plane.

The page is `transit.html`. The data is `data/transit.json` (hand-authored) and
`data/transit-schedules.json` (generated by `tools/fetch_transit.py`). The logic
lives in `assets/js/core/transit.js`.

## Why the curated file is the backbone

Feed coverage is inverted relative to need, which is the single fact that shapes
everything else here.

The services that are easy to look up are the ones that barely need looking up:
the Chamonix valley buses run every half hour, and the Swiss lines run to a clock
face. The services a bail-out day actually depends on — the Les Chapieux navette,
the Bourg-Saint-Maurice shuttle, the free Val Ferret and Val Veny buses out of
Courmayeur — are seasonal, a few runs a day, cash only, and published as a PDF
map or a paragraph on a tourist-office page. Two of them sell out.

So every service is a curated record with a season, a frequency or a departure
list, a fare and a phone number. The feed is enrichment on top: it replaces the
times and names the exact stop for the subset it covers, and changes nothing
else. A service is a usable graph edge whether or not a feed has ever been
downloaded.

## Confidence levels

Rendered on every journey, every service and every taxi option. Defined once in
`CONFIDENCE` in `assets/js/core/transit.js`, and mirrored in `fetch_transit.py`
with a test asserting the two agree.

| Level | Means | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `scheduled-2027` | A published 2027 timetable. Trust the minutes. | Only ever set by `fetch_transit.py`, from a feed whose own validity window covers the query date |
| `pattern-2026` | The 2026 season's shape standing in for 2027. Right buses, wrong minutes. | Hand-recorded from an operator's own 2026 timetable |
| `estimate` | Reconstructed from prose, a forum post or a trail guide. Indicative only. | Hand-recorded where no operator timetable could be found |

A journey shows the **worst** level among its legs, not an average, so a solid
itinerary cannot hide one unverified connection inside it.

As committed, the hand-authored file holds 24 services across 31 places and 13
operators: 15 `pattern-2026` and 9 `estimate`. Six declare feed coverage and four
of those are upgraded to `scheduled-2027` by the current extract.

## Sources and licences

Attribution is a condition of use for both feeds, not a courtesy. The page
renders each feed's licence and attribution line from
`data/transit-schedules.json`, and a test fails if a source is missing either.

| Source | Used for | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| [opentransportdata.swiss](https://data.opentransportdata.swiss/en/dataset/timetable-2027-gtfs2020) — Swiss timetable 2027 GTFS | Real July 2027 times for the Val Ferret bus, the Champex bus, the Forclaz PostBus and the Mont-Blanc Express | opentransportdata.swiss terms of use, attribution required |
| [transport.data.gouv.fr](https://transport.data.gouv.fr/datasets?q=chamonix) — Chamonix Mobilité GTFS | Chamonix valley buses, once a summer file exists | Licence Ouverte / ODbL, per the dataset page |
| Operator timetables and tourist-office pages | Everything else | Read, not redistributed; each service carries its own `source` URL |

Every curated service and on-demand option carries a `source` with a URL and a
`verifiedOn` date. That is what makes the file re-checkable rather than a set of
numbers someone once believed.

## What the feeds actually cover

Verified by running `tools/fetch_transit.py --date 2027-07-14` against the real
feeds. The gaps below are findings, not to-dos.

**Switzerland — the 2027 timetable is genuinely published.** Downloaded at 58 MB,
valid 13 Dec 2026 to 11 Dec 2027. Four services come back with published times:
the Val Ferret 272 (Orsières to Ferret in 27 minutes, ten runs), the Champex 271,
the Col de la Forclaz PostBus, and the Mont-Blanc Express hourly at :14 from
Martigny.

**Three Swiss things the feed does not give us:**

- **The 12.230 Forclaz–Trient–Le Châtelard summer bus is not in it.** The annual
  feed carries no route serving Col-de-la-Forclaz except the year-round PostBus
  213; seasonal additions are published separately. Both directions stay
  hand-recorded from the TMR PDF.
- **The St-Bernard Express is modelled as a shuttle.** Route R82 runs Orsières to
  Sembrancher and back, nine minutes, and the Martigny portion is a separate
  trip. The journey is sold as one and takes about 25 minutes, which is what the
  curated record says, but expect a platform change at Sembrancher.
- **Only the Swiss half of the Mont-Blanc Express.** The feed has Martigny, Le
  Châtelard and Vallorcine; Argentière and Chamonix are French and absent. Those
  two stops keep their hand-recorded offsets — see "splicing" below.

**France — the Chamonix file is the wrong season.** The current published file is
valid 7 Aug to 18 Dec 2026, a winter timetable, so a July 2027 query finds zero
active services and the tool says so. A summer file appears around June each
year. Two further quirks are handled in code: the feed writes single-digit lines
as `01` and `02`, and Swiss timetables write `12.272` where the feed says `272`.

**Italy — no open feed exists.** The Val Ferret "Linea Verde" and Val Veny
"Linea Arancione" are published as a PDF map. They stay hand-curated, and that
is a fact about Italy rather than something left undone.

**No feed at all:** Les Chapieux navette, Bourg-Saint-Maurice shuttle, Geneva
airport transfers, taxis.

## Splicing, and why it cannot shorten a line

`fetch_transit.py` overwrites the offsets for the stops a feed covers and leaves
the rest of the curated stop list alone. It does not replace the stop list.

This matters for exactly one service today and it is an important one. The
Mont-Blanc Express is what ties the Swiss and French sides of the loop together.
If the extract replaced its stops with what the Swiss feed knows, the line would
silently end at Vallorcine and no journey from Trient to Chamonix would exist any
more — a change that breaks a feature while every test about times still passes.
So the curated list stays authoritative on where a vehicle goes, and the feed
only corrects when it gets there.

Two guards, both tested:

- A feed pattern is only accepted if it starts at the service's first stop and
  visits the rest in order. Order is also what tells the two directions of one
  line apart.
- After splicing, offsets must still increase. If they do not, the whole entry is
  dropped with a note rather than written, because a leg that arrives before it
  departs would break the journey search.

Where several feed stops fall inside the match radius of one place, the nearest
wins. Ferret and Le Clou/Les Granges are a kilometre apart on the same bus and
picking the wrong one costs an hour on foot.

## Skipping a hiking day

A test asserts every hiking day can be got round by public transport within three
changes, because that is the promise the "Skip this day" link on each day page
makes. What it does not assert is that doing so is pleasant, and for two days it
very much is not.

Figures below are what `transit.dayOptions()` computes for the booked dates, so
they will move if the itinerary does. "Set off by" is the latest departure that
still arrives the same day.

| Day | By public transport | Fastest | Set off by | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Les Houches to Les Contamines | Bus into Chamonix, train to Le Fayet, Y84 up | 3 h 40 | 17:40 | Doable |
| 2 — Les Contamines to Les Chapieux | Y84 to Le Fayet, train to Bourg-Saint-Maurice, navette up | 8 h 15 | 09:00 | Call the taxi |
| 3 — Les Chapieux to Courmayeur | Navette down, train to Le Fayet, train to Chamonix, tunnel coach | 10 h 25 | 10:50 | Call the taxi |
| 4 — Courmayeur to Champex-Lac | Tunnel coach, Mont-Blanc Express, St-Bernard Express, bus up | 7 h 37 | 10:35 | Three changes, all morning |
| 5 — Champex-Lac to Trient | Bus to Orsières, train to Martigny, train to Le Châtelard, bus | 3 h 40 | 11:47 | Doable |
| 6 — Trient to Chamonix | Bus to Le Châtelard, Mont-Blanc Express | 1 h 36 | 16:58 | Easy |

Days 2 and 3 cross a range the road goes the long way round, so the loop by rail
is genuinely most of a day. Taxi Aarthur covers both, and both pairs are listed as
on-demand options so the phone number is next to the timetable rather than buried
somewhere else.

Adding the Le Fayet to Bourg-Saint-Maurice train is what made day 2 possible at
all; before it, the graph had no way round the Beaufortain and the page said there
was none.

Day 4 joined this list when the La Fouly night was dropped and the old days 4 and
5 became one. Walked end to end it is 48 km, and got round by vehicle it is three
changes that all have to happen before lunch — which is the argument for the
shortcut variant rather than for either extreme.

The deadlines on days 1 to 4 all rest on services marked `estimate`, so they are
the figures to re-check before travelling. Both **beds** off the trail, by
contrast, are reached on published or patterned timetables — a test asserts that,
because it is the half of the picture that would strand someone if it changed.

## Sleeping somewhere other than where the day ends

Two nights are not in the place the day's walking finishes, and the ride to them
is not optional:

| Night | Day ends at | Bed is at | How | Set off by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 4 | Les Chapieux | Bourg-Saint-Maurice, 13 km down the valley | Navette, ~45 min, about €10 | 18:20 |
| Jul 8 | Trient | Martigny, over Col de la Forclaz | Walk up to the col, PostBus down | 19:07 |

There is a third leg of the same shape pointing the other way: the group sleeps in
Chamonix the night before day 1 and starts walking at Les Houches, so the first
morning opens with Chamonix Bus line 1. `dayOptions()` models all three
identically — `toTrail` in the morning, `toBed` in the evening — rather than
special-casing the arrival, because they are the same problem and the page should
not have to know which is which.

Two things about these numbers are easy to get wrong, and both were got wrong
first:

- **A deadline is not the latest result of one search.** `journeys()` answers
 "leaving after this time, what arrives soonest", so its results cluster at the
 front of the day. Taking their maximum reported the last navette down from Les
 Chapieux as 10:50 rather than 18:20 — a figure that would have someone
 abandoning a day's walking before lunch for no reason. The departures are
 enumerated by stepping through the day instead.
- **Step past the vehicle, not past the set-off.** A leg that begins with a road
 walk can be started at any minute, so stepping one minute past a walk start
 finds the same bus again: that counted 80 imaginary departures a minute apart on
 the Trient leg and still never reached the last one. What is scarce is the bus,
 so that is what the enumeration steps over — and what is reported is the latest
 time you could set off and still make it, which on that leg is a full hour
 earlier than the bus itself.

The confidence shown against a deadline is read off the journey that sets the
deadline, not off the row. On the Trient leg these genuinely disagree: the quickest
way down is a 2026 pattern while the last one of the day is a published 2027
timetable. Describing the deadline with the fastest journey's confidence would put
the wrong label — in either direction — on the one number that can leave somebody
stranded.

## Booking deep links

`assets/js/core/links.js` expands templates stored next to the operator in
`data/transit.json`, rather than building URLs in the page. These rot: a renamed
query parameter still opens, still looks right, and quietly searches for the wrong
thing. Keeping them in data means a rotted link is a one-line fix, and each
carries the date it was last checked.

| Operator | Prefills | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CFF/SBB, TMR, PostAuto | From, to, date, time | SBB's partner deep-linking guide. Covers the eight Swiss services, whoever drives the vehicle. |
| FlixBus | Nothing | See below. |
| Everyone else | Nothing | Operator or route page, plus a Maps link that does know both ends. |

`expandTemplate()` returns nothing at all rather than a URL with a literal
`{to}` left in it, because a half-expanded template is a broken link that looks
like a working one and would be shipped by anyone who only glanced at the page.

**Why FlixBus has no template.** It prefills from numeric city ids, and the only
ids findable for Chamonix and Courmayeur came from a 2020 URL capture with nothing
to confirm which city each one is. A wrong id searches between two arbitrary cities
and looks like it worked, so the two tunnel-coach services point at their own
verified route pages instead. If someone confirms the ids from a live search, the
template can be added to the operator and nothing else needs to change.

**Why every leg gets a Maps link too.** Half the `booking.url` values in the data
are company front pages, which are not an action: they leave the reader to
re-enter the two places and the date they had already chosen. `legActions()`
therefore always offers transit directions alongside, since those are prefilled
even when the operator offers nothing, and a test asserts no ride leg is left with
an unfilled front page as its only option. `isBareHomepage()` is what tells the two
apart.

The Maps links deliberately carry no departure time: the documented Google Maps URL
parameters do not include one, and the undocumented parameter that does is a base64
blob that would break silently. Better to open on the right pair and let the reader
set the time than to look precise and not be.

Selecting a journey expands it in place rather than filling a panel a tap away.
Each ride names the stop to stand at (`stopName` where it differs from the trail
name), offers walking directions to that stop, a booking button, and a Maps link
for the ride itself. Only a prefilled booking earns a primary button — a front
page still labelled "Book" would be the original complaint. Departure times in
the service panel are buttons that set the planner's "leave after" time; they
looked clickable when they were plain text, and doing nothing was worse than
not showing them.

Granges is a kilometre below it.

## Running the pipeline

```
python3 tools/fetch_transit.py                      # cache first, all feeds
python3 tools/fetch_transit.py --date 2027-07-14     # basis date for the times
python3 tools/fetch_transit.py --only tmr-272-orsieres-ferret --force
python3 tools/fetch_transit.py --offline             # cached zips only
```

Raw zips land in `tools/cache/gtfs/`, which is gitignored — the Swiss feed is
58 MB and expands to gigabytes of `stop_times`. The extract it produces is about
5 KB and is committed.

Which services can be enriched is declared in the data, not the code: a service
with a `gtfs` block names its feed and enough to identify its route and
direction. Stops are matched to places by proximity, so a feed renumbering its
stop ids costs nothing. Adding coverage means adding a `gtfs` block, not editing
Python.

`--date` defaults to the trip's own start date rather than today, because a
seasonal summer service asked about in February comes back empty and looks like a
broken pipeline.

Two tests cover this. `tools/test/check_transit_pipeline.py` runs the extractor
against a synthetic feed with a deliberate short-working, so the direction,
proximity and splicing logic is checked without a download. `run-tests.js`
applies the same structural checks to the committed extract as to the
hand-authored file.

## Before travel

Do these in order. The trip is eleven months out and every French and Italian
seasonal timetable will be republished before then.

1. **Re-run the Swiss extract.** `python3 tools/fetch_transit.py --force --date`
   with a real trip date. The feed refreshes semiweekly and the 2027 file is
   already the right year, so this is the cheapest accuracy win available.
2. **Wait for the summer Chamonix file and re-run.** It appears on
   `transport.data.gouv.fr` around June. Until then the valley buses are a 2026
   pattern; check the printed timetable at the gare routière on arrival.
3. **Confirm the Italian buses by email or phone.** Arriva Valle d'Aosta
   republishes the free Val Ferret and Val Veny shuttles each June as a PDF.
   Update `val-ferret-924` and `val-veny-931` by hand and bump `verifiedOn`.
4. **Book the Les Chapieux navette the evening before.** Cash at the machine
   beside Auberge de la Nova. It sells out. The machine shows seats left.
5. **Check the Bourg-Saint-Maurice quay.** Sources disagree between 5 and 7. Ask
   at the station rather than trusting either.
6. **Re-verify everything still marked `estimate`.** Nine services, and in
   particular the two SNCF journeys and the Col de la Forclaz PostBus weekend
   times, all of which are multi-leg or reconstructed from a trail guide rather
   than read off a single timetable. The page's provenance panel counts them.
7. **Sanity-check the fares.** They are recorded for budgeting, not as a promise,
   and the Carte d'Hôte discount changed between 2025 and 2026 — the caveat on
   `chamonix-bus-1` explains what to expect.
8. **Open one SBB deep link and check it lands filled in.** The template is taken
   from SBB's partner documentation but could not be opened from the machine it
   was added on, so `verifiedOn` means "checked against the documentation", not
   "seen working". One tap settles it, and a failure is a one-line data fix.
9. **Re-check the two ride-to-bed deadlines on the ground.** 18:20 down from Les
   Chapieux and 19:07 out of Trient are the two times on this trip that can leave
   somebody without the room they paid for. Both come from timetables the group did
   not publish.
10. **Save the pages offline.** The service worker caches `transit.html` and both
   data files, but only after the page has been visited once on that device. Do
   it on hotel wifi, not in the Val Ferret.

Phone numbers are stored in dialable international form and a test enforces it,
so a `tel:` link works from any of the three countries.
